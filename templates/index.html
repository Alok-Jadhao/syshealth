          itself loads memory, not CPU.

          number states are judged on; Worst 10 s the
          sharpest spike. Load levels use the average pressure while each was
          held, skipping its first 10 s while the reading settles.

        What the number means
        
          
          
          
            The number is the share of time programs on the machine were held up
            waiting for memory. The Linux kernel measures it directly
            (PSI). It is not how
            full memory is. A machine can be 90% full and fine. What matters is
            whether programs have to wait. 20% means that out of every 10 seconds,
            2 were lost waiting, so the work could have run about 20% faster with
            enough memory.
          
          
          
            
          
            
            A state changes only after it has held for 15 seconds, so one spike is
            not reported as a failure. The thresholds come from systems that act on
            this measurement in production: Android’s low-memory killer,
            systemd-oomd and Meta’s oomd. Sources are in
            docs/thresholds.md.
